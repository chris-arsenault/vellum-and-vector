# Fire

![A geothermal country: lava veins running through cracked ground, a miner cracking a core at a vent, a river carrying its warmth down into autumn forest](../images/fire.webp)

_The hearth that can be carried._

**Physical character.** Fire is energy stored in minerals. A fire-core is a dense stone that holds
concentrated heat and releases it when cracked. Cores have two origins. Vein cores form where
geothermal heat rises through fractures beneath the land. While these conduits remain open, the vein
produces more cores and warms the ground above. Fallen cores arrive from outside the world in
impacts. They form a finite stock scattered around the crater, with fragments carried downstream in
river gravel. They contain metal from outside the world, and deposits of them interfere with
compasses.

**Where it is found.** Vein Fire occurs at geothermal hotspots beneath land. It does not form under
the sea floor, where geothermal heat produces ordinary vents. Its zones of warmth follow branching
fractures. Fallen Fire occurs in craters, in downwind fans of impact debris and in river gravels
downstream, where miners pan for fragments.

**Effects on the world.** Fire heats the ground, melts ice and hardens the rock it passes through.
Growth, seeding and decay accelerate around a core, and rivers carry the warmth downstream. Its
biomes are the Ember grove near the core and the Thaw meadow along the ice-melt boundary. Flow opens
a vein's conduits until its supply is spent, after which they seal. The ground cools, and towns
built around the vein may be abandoned. A fallen-core field has a different pattern: a crater lake,
a fan of impact debris that remains barren for a long time, and stretches of river gravel rich in
core fragments.

**Practice.** Fire practitioners call their work hearth-keeping. Their first skill is banking a
core: opening it gradually enough to warm a house for a season or a forge for a day. They also learn
to crack it for a sudden release that can cut stone, collapse a tunnel or break a wall. Both
techniques require holding and striking the core by hand. The release passes through the body, and
every use burns. Occasional use reddens the hands; a career leaves them mottled and stiff, with
locked joints and cracks that no longer heal. Skilled practitioners can judge how well someone
controlled a release by the burns it left.

**Lore.** Fire traditions hold that a house is wherever its coals are. When a community moves, it
carries live coals from the old hearth in a covered pot. The hearth's keeper is believed to travel
with them and is greeted at the new home with bread and salt. Leaving a fire behind is considered
terrible luck. A sealed vein is understood as an abandoned hearth: cold, with its keeper still
inside. When a town learns that its vein is nearly spent, its residents begin choosing which cores
to take with them.

Fire tales speak of deaths hidden in nested containers. A core's heat is hidden in the same way:
stone inside vein, vein inside mountain. A miner must open each layer to reach it. A core is cracked
rather than burned because its heat is held at the centre; the venting pick finds the point that
releases it. Fallen cores trouble this account. They come from another hearth, without a keeper.
Some schools refuse to use this strangers' fire. Others adopt it as they would a foundling. A smith
who works fallen metal can be recognised by the colour of the blade.

_From a smith's letter home._

**Interference.** Fire and Aurora cancel where they meet, and the line of cancellation is a thermal
rim of cracked ground. Fire released near a practitioner's stored Pressure charge destroys both
charges. Fire boils Greenwood's springs, fracturing the ground with steam, and accelerates its
growth until it rots. Fire burns Salt ground clean. Opening a Fire conduit breaks a Hallowed ward
line; releasing Fire ruptures a Veil door.
