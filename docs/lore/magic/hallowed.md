# Hallowed

![Pilgrims on a kept road toward a white shrine city on a hill, ruins and set stones along the way, a keeper's lamp burning in a wayside cell](../images/hallowed.webp)

_The ground on which nothing is harmed._

**Physical character.** Hallowed is created by communities. It accumulates where people gather over
generations, especially at old centres of settlement and pilgrimage sites. It also holds along
boundaries they bind through practice: walls, borders and riverbanks. It travels along regularly
walked routes and is stored in crystals and placed stones at shrines. Abandoning a shrine or ceasing
to walk a boundary lets it decay.

Within Hallowed ground, other systems weaken. Fire halos cool, Salt stops at the boundary, Veil
doors seal and storm tracks turn aside. Boundaries also stabilise the landscape: rivers keep their
beds and coasts stop eroding. Over time, the difference between the two sides becomes visible from
the air. The forces held back inside accumulate along the outer edge.

**Where it is found.** At old settlements and sanctuaries, along pilgrimage roads, and beside
maintained walls and borders. Ruins retain a fading field long after their caretakers leave. Its
distribution records where communities have maintained these practices over time.

**Effects on the world.** Hallowed protects crops and inhabitants by stabilising rivers and coasts
and suppressing other systems' hazards. Those hazards concentrate at its edge, making the land just
outside a great sanctuary unusually dangerous. Hallowed creates no biome of its own. It preserves
the biomes already within its boundary.

**Practice.** Hallowed practitioners take vows to minimise harm within a boundary. The full vow
forbids digging, kindling or extinguishing fire, damming water or drawing more than is needed,
stirring the air, and cutting living growth. Specialists observe it by travelling on foot, sweeping
the path before them, covering their mouths when speaking, and owning nothing that requires the
ground to be opened. Households follow a partial vow, reducing the harm they cannot avoid. The
traditions recognise that a household cannot meet every restriction of the full vow.

Consecration applies the vow to a place; a ward line applies it to a boundary. Placed stones mark
where it begins. The restrictions make leaving difficult. A full-vow specialist cannot ride, dig,
kindle or carry loads, so travel is slow and confined to maintained paths. A community that binds
its border must keep walking it for as long as it wants the ward to hold.

**Lore.** Hallowed traditions count earth, water, fire, air and plants among living things. Each is
said to possess one sense, touch, and each can be harmed. Followers understand the other systems
through the injuries they cause. Fire burns living earth; Pressure crushes living water. Salt kills
plants, Aurora stills them, Greenwood crowds them, and Veil tears the ground. To them, Hallowed is
the result of refusing those harms.

Their teaching explains a sanctuary's calm through everyday acts of restraint: leave the ground
undug and no Fire conduit is opened; leave water undammed and no still pool feeds a Pressure storm;
leave plants uncut and Salt has no new dead growth to claim. These are the tradition's explanations
for why the vow works. Its ideal is expressed in a simple promise: within this boundary, nothing
will be hurt.

The traditions also acknowledge what happens beyond the boundary. Households must do some harm to
live, and much of that work moves outside the sanctuary. Followers see this as the social
counterpart of the dangerous rim. They owe their neighbours for the harm displaced onto them. Moving
a boundary stone is therefore a grave offence. It disturbs the earth and moves the burden of harm
onto someone who did not agree to bear it. The vow is broken twice, and the ground held by that
stone loses its Hallowed protection.

> Come in. Eat. Sleep without fear.
>
> Tomorrow, take bread to the houses beyond the wall. Ask them what the weather was like.

_A sanctuary keeper's welcome._

**Interference.** Hallowed suppresses other systems within its boundary and concentrates their
effects at its edge. Opening a Fire conduit breaks a ward line; Aurora reinforces one. A Hallowed
boundary redirects Pressure storms, halts Salt and seals Veil doors. Protection inside increases the
danger outside.
