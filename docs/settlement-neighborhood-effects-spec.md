# Neighborhood effects: redesigned resources and verbs

Nov 19, 2026 · @Chris

This repository copy incorporates the owner's later timing corrections for **Eases** and **Raises**. It supplies the neighborhood-effects contract for town development. The related feedback design follows the effects through successive rounds and describes productive traditions. [Settlement feedback design](settlement-feedback-design.md)

## What this is

This specifies the resources and verbs for the neighborhood-effects model. It was tested against the 41 place families in `docs/TOWN-DEVELOPMENT-PLAN.md`, and each family is written out in the new vocabulary at the end.

It follows the rule that plan already sets for influences: each receiving process owns its question, and sources contribute to it. The decision owners, origins, carriers and productive traditions in the plan are unchanged.

Four changes:

1. **Presence is not an effect.** A place does not declare what it is. It declares what it does to other places.
2. **Five town media replace eight keys.** Each travels its own way and composes its own way.
3. **Eleven verbs replace four.** Each is a contribution to one process's decision, named for its outcome.
4. **Magic is seven media the world already carries.** The town reuses each one separately and never sums them.

## Why the original felt flat

"A reading room supplies learning" restates that a reading room exists. Most Supply clauses in the plan had that form: a workshop supplies craft presence, a market supplies exchange. The system already derives presence without any clause. `Conditions` carries households, institutions, workshops, exchange and density.

The four verbs also all ended at a number. Supply and Draw add and subtract, and Amplify and Dampen multiply and divide. No verb changed what could be built, what could improve, or what ground stayed open.

The town plan already lists what a contribution can do: add a burden, change a cost, establish a connection, offer an opportunity, restrict a choice, or break an ordinary rule. The verbs below are those, made specific.

## Media

A medium is something a place puts out beyond its own walls. The test is that places of several different activities produce it as a by-product. A temple, a hospital and a goldsmith all teach, and none of them is a school. Five media are authored on town places. Seven more are magical, already exist in the world, and have their own section below.

| Medium | Kinds | Travels by | Composes as | Read by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patronage | Townsfolk, countryfolk, travelers, wealthy | Routes from where that clientele lives, lodges or arrives | Shared out. A clientele is finite, and places that attract it compete for it. | Movement, livelihood |
| Nuisance | Smoke, noise, foulness, disorder, stigma | Exposure, differently per kind (next table) | Added up per kind | Occupation, maintenance, sanitation, security |
| Service | Clearance, protection, care | Service reach from the provider's entrance | Best provider in reach counts once. Capacity is limited. | Sanitation, security, occupation |
| Prestige | None | Membership: the same frontage or the same enclosure | Each kind of source counts once. Lasts while the fabric stands. | Occupation, investment |
| Learning | None. Sources are told apart by family. | People who come to learn or consult, on foot from the entrance | Each kind of source counts once. Builds with tenure. Sharing does not use it up. | Livelihood, investment, occupation |

**Nuisance has five kinds because five things drive places apart, each in its own direction.**

| Kind | Travels by | Countered by | Typical sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Smoke | Wind | Distance, walls | Smithy, brewhouse, ovens, bathhouse |
| Noise | Distance | Distance, enclosure | Yards, taverns, markets, fairs |
| Foulness | Water and ground, downstream | Clearance | Tannery, butcher, sanitation site, stables |
| Disorder | Routes people use at night | Protection | Taverns, landings, fairs, vice |
| Stigma | Sight and frontage | Enclosure | Gaol, burial ground, noxious works |

**Derived reads are not authored.** Processes keep reading them as they do now.

- Presence by activity: households, workshops, institutions, trade.
- Passing trade: exchange, through-traffic and traffic. These follow from where Patronage is attracted, since journeys pass other doors.
- Freight access, quiet, density, waste and decay.

Density, waste and decay are the negative side of presence. With the five nuisance kinds, decline has eight causes instead of one.

**From the plan's eight keys.**

| Plan key | Here |
| --- | --- |
| Exchange | Derived. It is the passing trade that Patronage journeys create. |
| Patronage | Patronage, now with four kinds of clientele |
| Craft presence | Derived. It is the presence of workshops. |
| Prestige | Prestige |
| Learning | Learning. Worship houses, religious houses, healers, hospitals, fine craft and guild halls all teach, as well as learned premises. |
| Clearance, Protection | Kinds of Service, as `ServiceRole` already has them. Care is new. |
| Nuisance | Nuisance, split into five kinds |

## Verbs

Eleven verbs. Each names an outcome and contributes to a receiving decision.

| Verb | What it does | Process that reads it | Limited by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attracts (clientele) | Brings that clientele's journeys to its door. Their routes pass other doors on the way. | Movement, livelihood | The clientele is finite and shared |
| Burdens (kind) | Puts a nuisance on its neighbors | Occupation, maintenance, sanitation, security | Its counter, per kind |
| Serves (kind) | Offers a public service within reach of its entrance | Sanitation, security, occupation | Capacity |
| Dignifies | Lends prestige to places sharing its frontage or enclosure | Occupation, investment | Stigma on the same frontage |
| Teaches | Makes knowledge available to those who come to learn or consult | Livelihood, investment, occupation | Tenure. It starts small and builds while the place stands. |
| Connects (foot, freight, water) | Makes a kind of journey possible or cheaper | Movement | The route it sits on |
| Eases (family, requirement) | Lowers one named requirement for a later placement of that building family or other placement class within reach | The receiving placement process, including siting or construction | The named requirement’s floor and the source’s reach |
| Bars (family) | Raises a requirement for that family nearby, up to excluding it | Siting, land competition | Its frontage or enclosure |
| Raises (family) | On successful placement of the source POI, raises an eligible building already present within reach | The development decision and fabric owner | A compatible existing target and an applicable improvement |
| Holds (ground) | Keeps ground open or reserved against encroachment | Land competition, collective investment | The strength of the claim |
| Keeps (system) | Sustains a magical system at this place by daily practice | Maintenance, and the system's own craft | Its keepers. It lapses when they leave, and the practice harms them. |

**Strength follows the state of the place.** A flourishing tavern attracts more and is louder than a struggling one. Trading state, occupation and condition already exist on each instance, so chains need no extra verb. Patronage received changes trading state, and trading state changes what the place puts out.

**Owners still decide.** Eases, Bars, Raises and Holds are evidence for the existing siting, investment, construction and competition decisions. No clause writes fabric.

**Raises changes an existing building on placement.** The source declares which class can be raised. When that source is successfully placed, the development owner selects an eligible existing target and the fabric owner applies the improvement. If there is no eligible target, the source can still be placed. A later round does not repeat the trigger for the same establishment. Use existing improvement operations where they express the change. Design a family-specific tier where the improvement needs a distinct built form or function. Means, trading state, building condition and the R01–R08 residential forms are separate facts.

**Eases stays available to later placements while its conditions hold.** Where the design limits how often a particular concession can be used, apply that limit to the named placement requirement. Raises follows the placement of its source.

## How it composes

Each medium has its own rule, because each answers a different question.

- **Patronage is shared out.** Places that attract the same clientele divide it by travel cost. A newcomer reads what is left. This is what spaces like uses apart.
- **Nuisance adds up per kind, then its counter is taken off.** Clearance reduces foulness. Protection reduces disorder. Enclosure reduces noise and stigma.
- **Service counts once.** A household served by two providers is no better served than by the nearer one. Capacity limits how many a provider serves.
- **Prestige counts each kind of source once.** A hall and a sanctuary on one square outrank two halls. Stigma on the same frontage is taken off.
- **Learning counts each kind of source once and builds with time.** A sanctuary, a hospital and a guild hall together teach more than three schools. It outlasts a closure while its community remains.
- **Bars beats Eases** where both reach the same placement. Requirements have a floor that Eases cannot remove.
- **Holds is contested.** Encroachment succeeds when the pressure on the ground exceeds the claim.

## Gather or space

A district forms when like places pull together at short range and compete at longer range. Each family needs both settings, and they are the main thing to author per family.

| Setting | Expressed as | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gather | Eases its own family, over a short reach | Fine craft forms a row, because skill and patrons are shared |
| Space | Attracts a clientele that others of its kind also attract | Taverns spread out, because townsfolk are divided among them |
| Keep apart | Burdens a kind another family cannot tolerate, or Bars it | Yards and formal residences end up on different sides of a wall |

Fine craft gathers strongly and competes weakly, since wealthy clients travel. Drinking houses do the reverse. A market does both: it eases traders onto its edge and it leaves little townsfolk Patronage for a second market nearby.

## Magic

Magic enters as seven media the world already carries. The atlas treats each system as a material with its own carrier. The town plan says to reuse those media and to avoid one generic magical bonus. So the town reads each system separately and never adds them into a single number.

Each system shows up in a town three ways: as a condition of the ground, as something places carry or cut, and as a practice that a keeping place sustains.

**The seven media at town scale.**

| System | Carried in a town by | Cut or drained by | What it does to the ground |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | Cores carried by people, open veins under the ground, and rivers taking warmth downstream | A spent vein. Fallen cores are a finite stock. | Warms it, melts ice, and speeds growth and decay |
| Aurora | Thick old ice, and portable crystals | Thin ice and warm ground | Cools it, slows growth and decay, and preserves |
| Pressure | Deep water, and a charge carried in the diver | Shelves, reefs and islands that break its rays | Fog, rich fisheries and storms, and crushed shores where rays converge |
| Greenwood | Connected vegetation, groundwater and springs | Roads, bare rock, open water and ice. An overdrawn spring collapses into sinkholes. | Deepens soil. Spring water heals and fertilises. |
| Salt | Brine seeping downslope and wind-blown crust. It forms at burial and killing grounds and at dried lakes. | Nothing removes the residue. The practice lapses without daily raking. | Thins vegetation, makes soil infertile, and preserves against decay |
| Veil | Nothing. It sits where two other systems tear space. | A missing house, post or keeper | A housed tear is a door. An unhoused one is ground that shifts between surveys. |
| Hallowed | Regularly walked routes, maintained boundaries, and stones at shrines | Abandonment, or a boundary no longer walked | Weakens every other system inside, steadies rivers, and piles the held-back hazards at its outer edge |

**Hallowed is the system a town makes for itself.** It accumulates where people gather over generations. Worship houses, shrines, religious houses, walked walls and old centres all feed it as a by-product. By the test in the Media section it is a town medium in the full sense. It builds with tenure and fades with abandonment.

**Greenwood turns streets into barriers.** It travels root to root, and a road cuts it. Greens, gardens, orchards and groves carry it through a town, so their continuity matters as much as their area.

**Practice.** A keeping place sustains its system by daily work. That is the eleventh verb, Keeps, and it lapses when the keepers leave. Every practice also harms its keepers, and that harm needs a place beside the work.

| System | Keeping place | What it does, in the verbs | Cost that needs a place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | Hearth-keepers with a banked core | Keeps Fire. Eases forges and cold-season occupation with heat that needs no fuel. Cracking a core eases building into rock. | Every use burns the hands, until a keeper needs help with ordinary tasks. |
| Aurora | Readers at the ice, or with crystals | Keeps Aurora. Teaches from the past held in the ice, and sees distant places as they are now. Attracts petitioners and those who want news. | Readers lose track of which memories are theirs. Schools rotate practice and rest, and retire readers early. |
| Pressure | Divers at deep water, and sky-readers ashore | Keeps Pressure. Connects by messages sent over long distances. Teaches the reading of weather. Attracts ships that want both. | Carriers grow distant and slow to answer while the charge stays in them. |
| Greenwood | Tenders of a grove, and healers at a spring | Keeps Greenwood. Holds connected planted ground. Serves care at the spring. Eases cultivation. A grove takes a century. | Tenders are bound to their land and sicken when it is cut. They do not relocate. |
| Salt | Rakers of a pan or burial ground | Keeps Salt, by raking every day. Holds the ground open. Teaches from the past held in it. Binds oaths and lays the dead to rest, so it Attracts families, claimants and courts. | Rakers cannot forget. Grief and oaths stay fresh. |
| Veil | Door-keepers with a house and a post | Keeps Veil. Connects to a distant partner, which makes an inland town a port. Attracts the partner's travelers and trade. Eases inns and storehouses. | Crossing leaves travelers and keepers unsure where they are. Lodging beside the door. |
| Hallowed | Vowed custodians of a shrine or boundary | Keeps Hallowed, by walking it. Bars work that digs, kindles, dams or cuts inside the line. Burdens the rim, where that work and the held-back hazards gather. Attracts pilgrims. | Custodians travel only on foot along kept paths. The walking never ends. |

**What must stand apart.** The systems interfere, and that sorts keeping places as strongly as nuisance sorts ordinary ones.

- A door cannot share ground with Fire work, Aurora crystals or a Hallowed ward. Fire ruptures it, Aurora closes it and Hallowed seals it. A stored Pressure charge cannot pass it.
- Fire and Aurora cancel, so a forge drains an ice archive.
- Fire boils a Greenwood spring and breaks a Hallowed ward line.
- Greenwood and Salt kill each other along their boundary.
- Aurora over Salt ground keeps the dead from resting.
- Two pairs help each other. Aurora reinforces a Hallowed ward, and Salt ground holds a tear open.

**Ordinary families on magical ground.**

| Family | System | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P08 Smithy | Fire, Hallowed | A banked core can replace its fuel. Kindling is against the Hallowed vow, so it moves to the rim. |
| P20 Court and P27 Burial ground | Salt | One institution on Salt ground. The burial ground holds the testimony the court hears. |
| P24, P25, P26 Worship house, shrine, religious house | Hallowed | They feed Hallowed by being used over generations. |
| P22 Gate, and town walls | Hallowed, Veil | A walked wall is a ward line. A door-house is guarded like a gate. |
| P28 Healer, P31 Water point | Greenwood | A healer works at the spring. Drawing too much water risks sinkholes. |
| P34, P35, P38 Green, garden, farmstead | Greenwood | They carry it across the town while they stay connected. |
| P30 Learned premises | Aurora | An archive reads the ice and keeps its written copies apart from it. |
| P16 Storehouse | Aurora, Salt | Both preserve against decay, which suits a store. This is an inference from the physical effect. |
| P17 Landing | Pressure | Fisheries, soundings and weather warnings gather at the shore. |
| P12 Inn | Veil | The inn beside the door lodges travelers who are still recovering. |
| P41 Magical practice premises | Any | The keeping place itself. |

None of this is a new magical power. The rows restate the seven lore files and `docs/settlement-lore-research.md` in this vocabulary. Each system's craft owns its answers, with the responder beside the system as the town plan requires.

**What the pass showed.**

- Every town verb is used by at least three families. Attracts is used by 22, Burdens by 19, Holds by 11, Eases by 10, Serves and Teaches by 8 each, Dignifies by 7, and Connects, Bars and Raises by 3 each.
- Keeps is used only by keeping places on magical ground. The Magic section lists the ordinary families that change there.
- Every Patronage and Nuisance kind has at least three sources. Clearance has two and stays because it already exists.
- Learning has eight sources across six different activities. Only one of them is a place of learning.
- Two families need no clause. Plain premises act through presence, which is as it should be.
- Families with the same verbs differ in their targets. A storehouse and a tailor both ease, but they ease different neighbors.

## Basis

What each source contributed to a choice above.

- **Dominion.** An engine needs cards in complementary roles: one that grants extra plays, one that draws, and a payload. Players sort cards by role, such as gainer, trasher and cost-reducer. That is why the verbs are roles with limits. [Engine](https://wiki.dominionstrategy.com/index.php/Engine) · [Glossary](https://dominionstrategy.com/glossary/)
- **Suburbia.** Tile text is keyed to the category of neighboring tiles, with both signs: a factory costs reputation for each adjacent civic or residential tile. The whole game runs on four currencies. That is why verbs name a target family and the media are few. [Rulebook](https://rulespal.com/suburbia/rulebook)
- **Farthest Frontier.** Shelters need high desirability to upgrade and are abandoned when it falls. Variety counts, duplicates add nothing, and a service building benefits a shelter once. That is the source of Raises and of the count-once rules. [Desirability guide](https://www.farthestfrontier.com/guide/villagers/desirability/)
- **Emilien et al., village generation.** Buildings are seeded by per-type interest functions, including attraction and repulsion at preferred distances between type pairs. That supports typed relationships over one desirability field. [Paper](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2012-villages.pdf)
- **Krugman and Marshall.** Centers form from a tension between forces that pull activity together and forces that push it apart. Trades cluster for a shared labor pool, shared inputs and shared knowledge. That is the gather-or-space rule. [Krugman](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4219) · [Marshall's three forces](https://cep.lse.ac.uk/_new/publications/abstract.asp?index=12215)
- **Medieval town regulation.** Tanners and butchers sent waste downstream, authorities controlled where trades could operate, and fire and night noise were regulated. That is the source of the nuisance kinds and of Bars. [Tanning](https://www.medievalists.net/2026/10/dirty-business-making-leather-middle-ages/) · [Urban regulation](https://popular-archaeology.com/article/inside-a-medieval-city-public-health-housing-and-everyday-regulations/)

* **The atlas's own magic model.** The seven systems are materials with their own carriers. The town plan says to reuse those media, to avoid a generic magical bonus, and to name the ordinary rule any new craft rule breaks. The Magic tables come from the seven lore files and the settlement lore research. `docs/adr/0007-magic-systems-are-materials.md` · `docs/lore/magic/` · `docs/settlement-lore-research.md`
