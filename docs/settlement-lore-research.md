# What the seven systems permit a settlement to do

The Canonry lore bible supplies a worked setting. Its upper terraces, crowded communal dwellings and lower vent works belong to one vertical colony, but different groups control light, trade and heat. The adjacent shelf settlement has acoustic chambers and a market operating under different authorities. A neutral transfer point exists because communities depend on materials and couriers from across a political boundary. At the Still Pools, an isolated community depends on vents that are failing. These are four different spatial relationships: dependency across levels, restricted knowledge of routes, exchange at a boundary, and a refuge whose concealment can decay. Each can survive translation without importing the setting's named peoples or its apocalypse. [Lore bible, §§2, 7–8, 11 and 16](../../the-canonry-game/docs/lore/lore-bible.md)

Ice in the lore bible can preserve impressions of events witnessed there. Readers can disagree about what those impressions establish. A court using ice testimony would therefore need room for independent readers, witnesses and deliberation. A single oracle that always gives the right answer would erase much of the institution. The three source disciplines also exact different costs from their practitioners. [Lore bible, §§4 and 7](../../the-canonry-game/docs/lore/lore-bible.md) · [Three Fires](../../the-canonry-game/docs/lore/encyclopedia/three-fires.md)

The source generation design places Fire along conducting fractures, Aurora in ice reservoirs, and Pressure in connected water shaped by basin geometry. Its interaction layer distinguishes a steam fracture from a thermal rim and from a coastal breach. A coast with two systems present need not have the same opportunities at every point. The atlas has its own seven-system material model; these older mechanisms explain why **the location and medium of a meeting** matter to a settlement designer. ## Settlement consequences proposed from the atlas lore

One cultural difference in the bible has a direct architectural consequence. In the Stack, witnesses give a deed standing; public recitation and records belong in inhabited places. On the Shelf, the last naming takes place in absolute darkness. Among the open-water communities, a warm vent can serve as neutral meeting ground without permanent buildings. A founder drawing on these precedents could open a court to maximize witnesses, build a room that excludes even stray lamplight, or maintain a recognized gathering place with no roof at all. Those decisions can give three communities distinct forms on equally suitable terrain. [Lore bible, §§5 and 12](../../the-canonry-game/docs/lore/lore-bible.md)

| System | Established condition or cost | Proposed place and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | A core can be banked for heat or cracked to shape stone. Handling it burns the practitioner. | A shelf settlement can cut a gallery through rock. A public warming room and lodgings for retired cutters can stand beside the working face. Access to a core and a crew matters to the next extension. [Fire](lore/magic/fire.md) |
| Aurora | Ice holds impressions. Reading takes time, and repeated readings erode the distinction between witnessed and personal memories. | An archive may keep its original evidence in the ice and its written interpretations in inhabited chambers. Reader rotations create traffic between them. Local heating threatens the collection. [Aurora](lore/magic/aurora.md) |
| Pressure | Deep descents let trained divers communicate by vibration or carry force. The diver must survive the descent. | A settlement can maintain sounding stations and a shore exchange. For permanent submerged homes the design still needs a credible inhabiting population or enclosed habitable space. A Pressure signal alone supplies neither air nor housing. [Pressure](lore/magic/pressure.md) |
| Greenwood | Tenders prepare soil and water for growth. A grove can take a century to mature, and tenders become bound to it. | Leave a watercourse and its rooting ground continuous through a settlement. When the grove predates the town, old trees can shape the kernel. A newly planted town must grow around young trees. [Greenwood](lore/magic/greenwood.md) |
| Salt | Raked pans and barrows require renewed labor. Salt preserves memory, including grief, and can bind an oath. | A memorial town can grow outward from the boundary between raked and untended ground. Roads may follow the causeways so daily passage keeps the pans intact. A public oath court needs visitors and witnesses as well as rakers. [Salt](lore/magic/salt.md) |
| Hallowed | Vowed custodians maintain a boundary and limit harm inside it. Restrictions bind the custodians themselves. Interference can intensify outside the rim. | A place of asylum can attract rivals who could never safely meet elsewhere. Repair yards and other incompatible uses can gather beyond the line. The religious decision to accept the vow is distinct from the ground's capacity to hold it. [Hallowed](lore/magic/hallowed.md) |
| Veil | A stable paired crossing needs a house, a post and a keeper. Crossings disorient travelers. Stored Pressure cannot pass, and Hallowed boundaries can seal doors. | An inland freight exchange can grow at a functioning door. Put waiting and recovery space on both approaches. A ceremonial ward around it could close the very route that supports the town. [Veil](lore/magic/veil.md) |

The right founding question is often specific: *is there a maintained crossing with somewhere to go?* or *does this grove already have mature roots?* A strong field is evidence for a medium. It cannot by itself establish a keeper, a mature institution, a people adapted to life underwater, a zealot government or an enslaved population.
