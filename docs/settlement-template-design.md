# Founding templates and major works

A template answers *who first lives here, on what ground, and under which rules can someone build next?* A major work answers *what extraordinary place stands here, who sustains it, and how does it affect its neighbors?* The two choices are independent. This is a candidate design for the shared Rust generator. Its sources are the [city, card and wonder research](settlement-reference-research.md) and the [atlas lore research](settlement-lore-research.md).

## Fourteen founding templates

The order groups related alternatives for reading. Suitability is ranked separately at each site. The founders need somewhere to live and a livelihood even when the most important part of the kernel is common ground, an agreement or a crossing.

| Template | Inhabited kernel | Rule during subsequent development | What a visitor can read on the map |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ferry commons | A ferryman's household, a working landing and scattered producers. | Both banks need a public approach. Later households can build far apart along incoming ways. A bridge can redirect the market and leave an old ferry lane behind. | One bank may grow far larger than the other; an abandoned inn can mark the former crossing. An ordinary inland road commons uses a junction in place of the ferry. |
| Cliff citadel | A defensible ledge, a guard household at its entry and suppliers below. | Supplies and heavy work need a route from the foot. Scarce defensible ground favors shared walls, upward building and guarded entries. A new shelf can acquire its own settlement. | A crowded upper refuge looks out over stores and loose dwellings on the approach. Walls follow defensible edges. |
| Town of two landings | Two usable levels, households at both and a crew operating a lift, stair or incline. | Every cross-level trip enters and leaves at actual landings. Repairs, tolls and competing stairs can shift which landing attracts work. | Someone directly below their destination may have a long journey. The lower town can be the wealthier one when it controls the transfer. |
| Moored flotilla | Inhabited hulls, one shared mooring and a shore supplier or reliable water approach. | New hulls add habitable surface with their occupants. Tie and gangway rights determine frontage. A departure can break a route; a grounded hull can become a permanent quay. | A floating neighborhood has seams, dead ends and detachable pieces. Landward streets can outlive the fleet. |
| Drowned borough | An inhabited submerged refuge, a surface exchange and a proven passage between them. | Submerged expansion needs habitable volume and access appropriate to its occupants. Transfers between wet and dry uses are scarce and can change hands. | A surface visitor first sees a few landing houses. The inhabited quarter and its streets lie below. Admit this template only with supported habitation at depth. |
| Door port | A maintained Veil link, a keeper household and an exchange at the threshold. | The paired destination supplies traffic. Keep waiting space near the post. Local streets can outlast a failed link, and another route may then support entirely different occupations. | Foreign freight and hostel yards can cluster on a short inland lane. The keeper's house is often smaller than the premises it supports. |
| Orchard of households | Tenders and cultivators occupy separate patches beside an existing spring or grove. | Shared roots and water must survive division of the ground. Reuse clearings, graft and plant over time. A dry or severed branch of the grove can lose its households. | The largest uninterrupted space is inhabited living ground. Houses can face inward from several edges without enclosing a conventional square. |
| Surveyors' ring | Founders set a center and bearings, occupy one arc and reserve a radial right of passage. | A charter governs the alignment of each new frontage. Arc segments can remain open for generations. Outside the charter, builders can follow older tracks. Strong enforcement on suitable terrain can produce a complete compass-oriented circle. | A compass bearing can tell a player where a missing gate *should* be, even where a ravine interrupted construction. |
| Sanctuary of the vow | An observant community, its custodians and an inhabited threshold where outsiders can receive assistance. | Obligations determine permissible work, where petitioners wait and who can sponsor extensions. A fervent congregation can dedicate substantial labor. Actual Hallowed effects require the corresponding practice. | A tranquil inner precinct can coexist with an intensely active outer rim. A schism can leave two rival approaches. |
| Walled household state | A ruling household claims a walled compound and settles dependent workers near the work and supply gates. | Tenure and permission determine who can move, build and benefit from improvements. Under slavery, compelled households may house themselves densely outside while elites reserve or clear land inside. Freed residents can later establish claims of their own. | A service entrance may connect more people than the ceremonial avenue. Guard posts, unofficial paths and autonomous outer streets reveal the distribution of power. |
| Republic of refuge | Refugee households occupy an old enclosure and agree on rights to shelter, trade and collective defense. | New arrivals can claim a court by joining the compact. The assembly controls common passages. Rival interpretations can split a ward or open a second refuge. | Successive communities occupy adjacent courts with independent gates and a shared assembly approach. Inherited walls may run through today's civic center. |
| Treaty market | Separate communities establish a neutral exchange, resident hosts and a protected path to each side. | Visiting parties can trade while retaining separate control of stores, lodging and onward routes. Expansion needs agreement on common ground. When trade breaks down the two residential sides can survive. | Two busy approaches may end in a single small neutral court. A courier can sleep on neutral ground without entering either jurisdiction. |
| City of witnesses | Rakers or readers live beside a burial ground or an ice record with a community that seeks their testimony. | Living households maintain witness access to a medium that must stay intact. Records can accumulate, be disputed, or become unreachable. Salt pans and Aurora repositories retain distinct practices. | Courts, lodging and processional routes can multiply around ground kept deliberately unbuilt. The dead may occupy the most valuable frontage in town. |
| Commonwealth of guilds | Workshops share an essential service and establish a hall where access and upkeep are negotiated. | Distinct bodies may own premises, control a service and admit apprentices on the same street. New trades can split off with a yard of their own. | The jurisdiction of a guild is easiest to find by following a conduit or delivery lane. Rival halls can face one another across shared machinery. |

The cultural designs above are possible founding arrangements. A fortress flag establishes neither enslavement nor religious fervor. When history actually identifies an institution or an existing population, preserve it. Where history leaves social organization open, selection may found a new one explicitly. A common settlement remains a credible choice in extraordinary terrain.

## Eight axes available for ranking

Use local evidence around the inhabited kernel and its plausible approaches. Sample both sides of an obstacle. A mean over the map would give a flat field with one sheer edge the same character as a uniform incline. These are founding observations composed from existing data; several need exposing earlier in the town adapter.

| Axis | Producer or source | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relief and habitable shelves | Real-scale field elevation and slope, candidate shelves in [development.rs](../crates/atlas-generation/src/atlas/town/development.rs) | Distinguish one defendable ledge, two usable levels and open ground. Slope alone proves none of these. |
| Water and landing geometry | Local field water, shore, flow and drawn water in [site.rs](../crates/atlas-generation/src/atlas/town/site.rs) | Locate usable shore, crossings, depth and separate landings. A coastal settlement label is only a coarse hint. |
| External access | Captured ways, destinations, arms and strategic approaches in [site.rs](../crates/atlas-generation/src/atlas/town/site.rs) | Compare a junction, a single defended entrance and opposed approaches. A road arriving twice from one direction is weak evidence for a treaty market. |
| Existing occupation and land use | Local population, cultivated and rural service conditions in [influence.rs](../crates/atlas-generation/src/town/influence.rs), read from the field | Distinguish an inhabited agricultural fringe from largely open founding ground. Retain workable land around a common. |
| Civic position and settlement history | Seat, guarded status, number of inhabited eras, headcount and built extent in [story.rs](../crates/atlas-generation/src/town/story.rs) | Public authority can sponsor charters. Evidence of later planned settlement favors deliberate alignment. Population also informs the separate major-work draw. |
| Existing institutions and landmarks | Located features and their catalog-owned offers in [providers.rs](../crates/atlas-generation/src/atlas/town/providers.rs) | A shrine, trading institution or guarded site can anchor a kernel. Keep location, identity and kind of offer. An area-wide institutional score cannot tell a founder which place is involved. |
| Magical medium and interference | The seven physical fields, located stocks and meeting features, described in [the atlas lore](lore/magic.md) | Prefer a real ice archive, brine pan, rooting ground or stable tear over mere nearness to a magical region. Preserve system identity throughout the choice. |
| Local practices and maintained connections | Craft evidence on the inhabited field; maintained door pairs exposed by [kept_links](../crates/atlas-generation/src/atlas/town/providers.rs) | Practitioners or a functioning door are stronger evidence than a physical opportunity alone. A keeper at one end cannot create a missing destination by selection. |

**Selection contract.** Each template declares indispensable capabilities and the evidence it favors. First exclude candidates whose kernel cannot physically operate. Rank the remainder by local suitability and retain each candidate's contributing evidence. Equal evidence gives equal weight. A positive baseline allows plausible alternatives to be drawn. As a first design for the sampler, give eligible candidates one base ticket, add two for each strongly matching independent axis and one for a supporting axis, then draw proportional to tickets. Count an underlying fact once. These weights illustrate the contract and can change; the creator can later choose directly from the identical ranked set.

For cultural templates, read the spatial relationship between offers. Two distinct trading communities approaching opposed sides of a neutral site favor a treaty market. Several workshops drawing on one located service favor a guild commonwealth. A sacred destination on a traveled route favors a sanctuary; an existing vowed circuit gives stronger evidence for its magical variant. A political seat with a defended precinct makes household rule plausible, but its wall says nothing about whether the people outside it are free. When that question is open, a coerced founding regime is an explicit authored possibility of the template. Where existing history specifies free households, honor those rights. Institutional variation should remain possible among sites with similar terrain.

**Constructed founding decisions.** Consider a guarded river cliff with two usable shelves, one trade road and a ferry. For the two-landings template, the two shelves strongly match and the trade approach supports it: four tickets. For the cliff citadel, ledge and guarded approach strongly match on separate relief and civic axes: five. For the commons, the ferry strongly matches: three. Among these three the odds are 4/12, 5/12 and 3/12 respectively; any other eligible template joins the same draw. The citadel would begin with the upper entry and suppliers below. The landings town would begin on both shelves and its first disputed parcel might be beside the lower hoist. On a flat, recently chartered inland plain the two shelf kernels are infeasible. The charter and open ground favor the surveyors' ring; nearby workshops and a shared approach also make a guild foundation plausible. A large population by itself decides neither result.

**Handoff.** Founding should hand the selected kernel's occupied sites, named relationships and continuing rules to ordinary development. Existing [founding](../crates/atlas-generation/src/town/founding.rs) establishes a household and workplace on candidate parcels. Its two-dimensional ground and the five existing layout labels cannot by themselves express wet occupation, overlapping levels, mobile habitable ground or rights of tenure. Those are explicit implementation dependencies. The template should request the relevant placement or access rule through the shared contracts; avoid template switches in movement and construction. Each round can still extend the town, build upward, change occupation, or leave parts of the kernel behind. The selection never draws a finished district.
