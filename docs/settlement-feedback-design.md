# How established places shape settlement growth

A creator should be able to pick out a market district, a noble compound or a working waterfront on a color-coded town map from the mix and form of its buildings, grounds and routes. The district becomes recognizable because occupied places change what happens around them. Later development can change their neighbors, their clientele and eventually the places that started the change. These effects matter from the first scattered premises through the redevelopment of an established city.

An established point of interest (POI) can shape that history in two ways. First, general verbs and resources give each use a short, legible description of what it does locally. Their composition can produce chains across unrelated uses. Second, authored productive relationships explain why particular activities specialize, take on different forms and congregate or spread apart. Both work through roughly eighteen growth rounds of comparable magnitude. Multiple places can develop in each round, and processes recur on their established cadence. Founding templates supply the kernel and persistent rules. Collective projects retain their purposes, and existing siting and construction rules decide what fits.

## 1. Neighborhood effects in development

The [neighborhood-effects specification](settlement-neighborhood-effects-spec.md) defines the five town media, eleven verbs, seven magical systems and family-level examples. Presence, including nearby workshops, is derived from what occupies the town. Authored clauses describe what those places change for others. Each receiving process owns how its answer combines sources. Use accessible paths, directional exposure or membership as defined by the medium.

For a carrier that attenuates with travel or exposure cost, one candidate response is $K(s,x)=\exp[-C(s,x)/L]$, with characteristic reach $L$. In open ground the directional cost can differ along and across the carrier, or upstream and downstream. For accessible service, $C$ follows a usable route from an entrance and is infinite if the recipient cannot reach it. Enclosure or frontage membership can directly determine reach for prestige. The carrier answers whether and how strongly an effect gets to a location. The relevant medium then determines whether sources add, compete or count once.

Eases lowers a named requirement for a later placement. Raises occurs at successful source placement and applies to an eligible building already present. The fabric owner applies the target's applicable improvement. Its result becomes available to later development reads.

### How a feedback loop closes

A use changes a local reading. At their ordinary read boundaries, livelihoods can change clientele and occupation, sanitation can remove waste, investment can adapt premises around a purpose, movement can change an approach, and construction can fit a new use. Each owner commits its own change. Later steps read the new fabric and later rounds can act on consequences that have accumulated. The original place participates in those same decisions. The pace depends on how quickly occupation, routes and investment change. Several paths can unfold together within one round, or a path can stall when its grounding conditions fail.

The following histories use sample clauses and supplied site conditions. The steps describe plausible decisions and the facts that would change those decisions. The clause declarations specify the source's effect. The receiving owners decide which later development changes fit.

**Care and learning can change the place that began the loop.** Suppose an occupied medical tent serves care and teaches through its public entrance, and a reachable washhouse serves clearance. Its strongest effects follow the lane into established housing. Sanitation reads the washhouse's clearance locally and removes waste, leaving that approach better able to sustain dense occupation. The learning can support teaching added to an available room or a school with its own entrance. A teaching use can become a second kind of learning source on the same accessible approach.

Households seeking instruction can favor that approach. Where wealthier households and visitors take up premises, their occupation and means supply patronage. Their preference for more dependable care can change the existing tent's clientele and the collective investment decision about permanent care. If the site, access and sponsorship fit, the tent's holding can become a permanent care court that serves more care and teaches, and dignifies premises sharing its frontage or enclosure. Otherwise the service could expand in shared rooms nearby. The resulting learning and prestige can also support another use such as a library, especially where a community with an existing teaching or record-keeping practice can sustain it. A gate closure, a school drawing from a different street or a cramped original site can leave the tent small or move the permanent institution to another approach. One outcome leaves dispersed care and teaching premises. Another leaves a public court, several institutional entrances and high-service houses around the same lane.

**Custom can concentrate, divide and move.** An open market attracts clientele along its public approaches. The resulting journeys contribute to passing trade. Busy frontages can add shop or common-room uses, and the occupied shops attract customers of their own. Repeat visits support a worn passage or an improved way through the ordinary movement process. Where a formal precinct meets one of these approaches, a court can dignify its frontage and visitors with means can contribute patronage. An inn that offers private lodging can flourish there, while a tavern by the landing serves workers and carriers with shorter visits. The difference can be visible in the inn's enclosed court, its formal entrance and the houses bordering it, and in the tavern's narrow working frontage and direct doors on the freight street. If an enclosed compound gains occupied premises inside, their means can support grounds and gate services. Protection along the inside routes can favor further investment there, while trade and hospitality spread along the public approach. A gate that closes to public journeys changes the operating ties. Commercial growth can turn down another lane while the court remains a landmark. If the market changes entrance, a formerly busy frontage can lose occupation and become available for another use as the new approach develops.

**The same use can draw one neighbor and drive out another.** A working yard contributes to derived workshop presence along approaches used by workers and burdens nearby ground with nuisance. The two reaches overlap only in part. A narrow passage can carry workers to the yard against the prevailing wind. An equally close residential court on the downwind side can experience smoke and have poor access to work. Livelihoods and maintenance can sustain occupation on the quieter approach while the downwind holding loses it. Exposed premises may take on storage, a compatible trade or a larger yard, which changes the extent of the nuisance and the pattern again. If the relevant counter reduces exposure or uses change their operations, reuse can favor housing again while existing lanes and sturdy yard buildings persist. The resulting industrial edge can be one-sided even where land on both sides was equally buildable.

## 2. Productive traditions and the districts they leave

Author what an activity makes possible for another activity. A **productive tradition** links activities through named useful things or capabilities. A link specifies the useful offer, what a receiver could do with it, its usable origins and routes, and the spatial consequence of working that way. A holding can perform several activities, and several premises can share one. An external destination can supply a link when an operating route connects it to an offer it can provide.

For an operation, first read its real constraints, such as water and drying space for wet finishing or a haul-out site for boat repair. Build the available alternatives for each link from actual providers that can reach that operation. Use ordinary operating access to distinguish foot, freight and water, including the cost of handling the thing at either end. Available alternatives within a link represent different ways of obtaining the same opportunity. The same partner reached by two routes is one offer. Different links are complementary opportunities and can support different expressions of a trade.

### Clothworking links

| Receiving operation | Opportunity and alternative providers | Access and handling | What the receiver can do with it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wet finishing | Unfinished cloth from household or workshop weaving, or an importer offering the same material | Dry loads can follow a walkable approach at household scale. Bulk deliveries favor a freight or water connection with room to unload. Wet finishing itself needs usable water, and open ground allows drying alongside it. | Finish cloth near the weavers where room permits, or develop a shared yard nearer water and a connection that can supply bulk work. |
| Cutting and decoration | Prepared cloth from a local finishing yard, a trader bringing in finished cloth, or a merchant stocking either | Prepared pieces travel on foot in smaller lots. A larger workroom can use freight without needing direct water access. | Retain these uses near patrons or skilled workers even where wet finishing takes place across town or outside it. |
| Decorative work for commissions | Demand for textile work from a court, a civic institution or an export buyer with a relevant trading connection | Samples and visits favor the court's public approach. Large finished orders can be dispatched through a freight partner. | Support finer work at an existing shop or room for a specialist in a separate premises. An alternative outlet can sustain the work after one patron leaves. |

Interpret a link as an identity for one opportunity, such as *obtain unfinished cloth*. For each matching partner, keep its best usable route for that operation and include any handling it needs. An occupied wet yard might start with nearby weavers. Loss of a pedestrian crossing makes their offer less useful, or unavailable if the crossing was their sole route. A reachable importer can then provide the same opportunity, with freight journeys taking the place of visits to the weavers. Retained custom can favor a familiar source while both are reachable. Its tie loses use when the partner can no longer supply it. If the importer offers only finished cloth, the local yard's opportunity disappears, while cutting and decoration still have a source. An independent buyer commission changes the audience for decorative work and can strengthen it alongside any viable cloth supplier.

Reuse, expansion and construction read these different operating possibilities in their existing decisions about premises. They can share work on scarce ground, split a large operation across yards, or put portable work close to its clients. Movement can improve a shared approach once repeated journeys use it. The link is evaluated at the scale of the operation and the partners it can reach. Accounting for individual orders and batches would add detail without determining any of these spatial alternatives.

### District form across trades

| Tradition | Authored productive relationships | Where the same relationships can lead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clothworking | Local weaving or imports can serve wet finishing. Prepared cloth, skilled hands and patrons can meet elsewhere. | A river town can develop a long bank of yards with open drying grounds. An inland seat importing prepared cloth can develop fine, narrow shopfronts near patrons. A larger city can sustain both, linked by carriers through otherwise residential streets. |
| Building stone | Extraction and salvage both offer workable stone. Rough shaping close to a bulky source reduces cartage. Prepared pieces travel more readily to masons serving local commissions. Skilled finishing can attach to a patron's site or serve many sites from one yard. | A quarry settlement can spread along a haul road with broad working courts. A city supplied by barge can concentrate yards at a landing. Sustained commissions can draw smaller finishing courts into a civic precinct while heavy work stays at the water. |
| Vessel building and repair | Timber preparation supplies structural work. Ropework and clothworking can serve rigging as well as other trades. Access to a shore suitable for hauling vessels favors repair even where hull components arrive from elsewhere. | A sheltered shore can support dispersed repair slips among fishing households. A freight landing can grow deep working plots behind a common launch frontage. Specialist suppliers may share an inland craft street with customers who have no connection to the harbor. |
| Hot metalwork | Metal can arrive as ore, salvaged stock or prepared bars. Primary work benefits from sites that can sustain high heat and heavy handling. Fitting and repair can use local output or imported stock. Fine metalwork can follow skilled partners and patrons with much less freight. | A wide bank can carry a row of heavy yards with freight approaches. A narrow shelf can favor one shared works with light fitting shops along the way out. A town supplied with bar stock can concentrate on repair and finery near customers, with little primary work. |

A city can sustain several branches of the same tradition because more customers, partners and workable sites overlap. A small port with reliable outside supply or demand can sustain concentrated specialization too. Changes in suppliers, clientele, route or workable ground can therefore produce markedly different forms at similar populations. The exaggeration should come from the spatial consequences themselves. Give repeatedly used drying ground enough depth to read from the map, shared stock handling enough room to produce a recognizable yard, or portable high-value work enough occupied frontage to distinguish a dense inland quarter. The links give those forms a cause and leave their extent, mixture and arrangement open.

### Where the two models meet

Suppose an existing landing gains a connection carrying roughly shaped stone. The productive relationship makes a stone yard at the landing plausible because the remaining heavy journey is short. Once occupied, the yard adds to derived workshop presence and burdens the surroundings with nuisance from its grounds. Those local effects reach households and unrelated trades as well as masons. A commission behind a gate might support a finishing court nearby, but most handling can stay at the landing. If the commission ends, the court loses that partner. Its valuable frontage, trained neighbors and access to other clients may still sustain a different use.

The stone relationship explains *which work can split between two sites*. Local resources explain *what each occupied site then does to the surrounding town*. The same relationship can produce a conspicuous bank of yards and a fine-grained civic fringe, or a single mixed-use holding. As neighboring work and routes change, they can feed back into the original yard's choice to expand, narrow its work, or give space over to another use.

**A Fire-core nexus illustrates an exceptional case.** Suppose a Fire craft makes a particular hot operation feasible at sites with usable worked heat. The craft would own the answer about that operation. Today Fire's hearth-keeping answers season and climbing, so usable industrial heat would need its own demonstrated rule and craft response. The settlement would read the answer for the actual premises and practitioners. Good freight, sheltered working space and reachable downstream work can make the first occupied works worthwhile even if imported metal already serves local buyers. It can add to workshop presence, burden its surroundings, offer prepared stock to other trades and help concentrate journeys to the landing. Once a route improves, another yard can use the remaining workable hot ground and reachable connections. On a narrow shelf the same relationships can favor one immense shared works with a trail of fitting shops. Without suitable freight or practitioners, the ordinary town can instead keep importing prepared stock and develop small repair premises. A strong magical presence alone says little about the visible size or arrangement of the district. ## Fit with the town generator

Occupied production premises already contribute nearby workshops. A smithy reads workshops alongside freight, traffic and rural activity. Fine craft combines workshops with patronage and protection. Clearance and protection already read reachable service entrances, while nuisance and workshop exposure use distance to the holding. Sanitation uses clearance to remove waste. These are existing routes from occupied premises through a local reading to a later decision. Several originate in a broad activity or service role. Put an adopted effect at its POI source and retire the corresponding generic contribution when that producer is adopted. Three gaps shape the proposal.

1. **Outgoing effects.** Adopt the spec's outgoing verbs and compose their contributions through the existing receiving questions. Distinguish passing trade from authored patronage and reconcile the existing exchange signal when adopting the new sources. Learning and local prestige are proposed conditions. Existing catalog prominence describes a place and is separate from the standing available to other places through access. [Site reads and exchange signal](../crates/atlas-generation/src/atlas/town/development.rs)
2. **Responses at existing premises.** Livelihoods already update operating ties, means and occupation. Investment can put an accommodation in shared or vacant premises. Maintenance responds to changing local exposure, and land competition can change the geometry of an occupied holding. Use these owners for the proposed effects on clientele, adaptation and decline. A reader needs to gain a specific local fact where it changes a decision. For example, an existing purpose can use local patronage and reachable care to compare investment in a better care space. The medical tent example also needs a way for a purpose to improve the origin holding. Current sharing and reuse proposals exclude the purpose's own anchor. That gap belongs to the investment and fabric decisions. ## Reading the result on the map

Color the realized premises by use and residential form, with district names and influence overlays hidden. Look for a shift in building mix and form as well as a concentration of uses. A market can read as several busy approaches, courts and occupied frontages. A noble compound can read as an enclosed group of formal premises and grounds, with agents and services developing along a different side of the gate. A working edge should be readable in the depth of its yards, its routes and the kinds of houses or other uses that border it. Follow an early, intermediate and mature view to see whether changes in routes or occupation reached existing premises, including the sources. Compare a related trade in towns with different ground and connections. The evidence is a family resemblance in the actual premises and large variation in where they gather, split and occupy space.
